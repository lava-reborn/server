# Passability of the global map, read off the map

Built by `data/build_terrain.py` from the 256 tiles of `res/globalmap/full` that cover the playable rectangle, 8x8 pixels a cell.
The server's own table is lost; this is a reconstruction, and the checks below are how far it can be trusted.

## The checks

**Settlements.** All 262 cells of `data/settlements.json` are passable. 16 of them the photograph alone would have walled in (an icon sits on the cell, or the place is built against a scarp); they are open because a player has stood there, not because the picture says so.

**Map tooltips.** All 414 cells of `res/database/locations.xml` are passable, 5 of them forced the same way. Blocked after forcing: 0.

Of the 21 cells forced open, 15 read as rock and 6 as water. The wet ones are shore: 5863,1946, 5879,2011, 5970,1727, 6017,2086, 6032,1951, 6185,1977 -- a place standing on the bank of the lake covers a cell whose 8x8 block is mostly water.

## Where to distrust this

* Cells under an icon or a caption are not measured at all, only filled in from their neighbours: 9625 cells of the map are more than a quarter painted over.
* The 1 settlements listed above sit in pockets the walker cannot reach. Some of that is real -- the valley is meant to be sealed -- and some is the picture being harsher than the game was.
* Roughness is a proxy for relief. A field of dark boulders reads as a mountain; a smooth dune field reads as a road. The threshold (26) was chosen as the loosest one that still closes the valley.
* Water is only Lake Nasser and the Nile: 0.65% of the map. Narrow stretches of the river are drawn thinner than a cell, so the river does not cut the map in two the way the real one does.
